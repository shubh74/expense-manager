1. Our approach

The safest data is data we never hold. Your ledger, messages and receipts stay on your phone, and your backups are encrypted before they leave it. What we do hold on our servers is kept to the minimum in section 3 of the Privacy Policy.

2. Security measures

  • Encryption in transit: every connection to our servers and Google uses TLS.
  • Encrypted backups: your backup is encrypted on your phone with AES-256-GCM before it is uploaded to your own Google Drive. We never receive the file. With a passphrase, only you can open it.
  • Keys on your phone: backup keys and passphrases are kept in Android's secure keystore. Your app-lock PIN is never stored, only a salted, stretched hash of it (PBKDF2), inside the app's private storage.
  • Least-privilege servers: our database rules let each signed-in person read and write only their own records. Nobody, including us, can make themselves an admin from the app. The rules are checked with automated tests when they change.
  • Minimal access: only a small number of authorised people at have administrative access, through their own Google accounts, and use it only when needed to run the Service.
  • App hardening: release code is obfuscated, the app lock supports fingerprint, and updates are signed with our private key, so Android refuses any update that is not genuinely ours.
  • Secure development: we keep dependencies up to date, review changes before release, and test each version before publishing it.
  • Logs: security and processing logs are kept for at least one year, as the DPDP Rules require, to detect and investigate misuse.

3. What you can do

  • Turn on the app lock in Settings, and keep your phone's screen lock on.
  • Use a strong, unique password or Google Sign-In.
  • Install updates when the app tells you, and download Paisook only from our website or an official app store.
  • We will never ask for your OTP, UPI PIN, card number or password. Anyone who does is not us.

4. Data breach response procedure

A personal data breach is any unauthorised processing, loss, use, disclosure or access that affects the confidentiality, integrity or availability of personal data. If we learn of one, we follow these steps:

  • Step 1, contain (immediately): stop the breach, revoke exposed keys or access, and preserve evidence.
  • Step 2, assess (within hours): find what data, how many people and which risks are involved, and its likely impact on you.
  • Step 3, CERT-In (within 6 hours): report cyber-security incidents to the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team, as its 2022 Directions require.
  • Step 4, tell you (without delay): notify every affected person in the app and by email, in plain language: what happened and when, what data was involved, the likely consequences, what we have done to reduce harm, what you can do to protect yourself, and who to contact.
  • Step 5, Data Protection Board (without delay, then within 72 hours): give the Board an initial intimation, then within 72 hours a detailed report of the facts, cause, mitigation, any findings about the person responsible, and the notices sent to you, as the DPDP Rules, 2025 require.
  • Step 6, GDPR (within 72 hours): if people in the EU / UK are affected, notify the competent supervisory authority within 72 hours, and those people directly when the risk to them is high.
  • Step 7, fix and learn: remove the root cause, review our safeguards, and record the incident, its effects and the actions taken in our breach register.

5. Reporting a security problem

If you find a vulnerability or suspect your data has been exposed, please write to the support email shown in Settings → Help & feedback with "Security" in the subject and as much detail as you can. We acknowledge reports within 48 hours. Please give us a reasonable time to fix a problem before making it public, and do not access other people's data while testing. We will not take legal action against anyone who reports in good faith and follows these rules.
